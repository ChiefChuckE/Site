## CALL 5 · The four signs
*Thu Nov 5*

Four signs. Learn them like your ABCs.  
  
One. It comes back. Nightmares. Flashbacks. A smell that drops you right back on scene.  
  
Two. You avoid. Certain streets. Certain calls. Talking about it at all.  
  
Three. Your mood and thinking change. Guilt. Numb. "The world is rotten." You stop enjoying what you loved.  
  
Four. You're always on. Jumpy. Angry. Can't sleep. Back to the wall.  
  
If those hang on for more than a month and get in the way of your life,  
that's not "just the job."  
  
That's a reason to get checked.

## CALL 28 · Five questions
*Sat Nov 28*

Five yes-or-no questions. Be honest. Think about the past month.  
  
One. Have you had nightmares about it, or thought about it when you didn't want to?  
  
Two. Have you tried hard not to think about it, or gone out of your way to avoid reminders?  
  
Three. Have you been constantly on guard, watchful, or easily startled?  
  
Four. Have you felt numb, or cut off from people and things you used to enjoy?  
  
Five. Have you felt guilty, or unable to stop blaming yourself or others?  
  
Three or more yeses? That's not a diagnosis.  
It's a reason to talk to a doctor. This week.  
  
These come from the VA's own screening questions.

## CALL 36 · Ask the question
*Sun Dec 6*

Ask it out loud. "Are you thinking about killing yourself?"  
  
Not "You're not thinking of doing anything stupid, are you?"  
That's asking for a "no."  
  
Ask it straight.  
  
You will not put the idea in their head. Research says asking doesn't raise the risk.

## CALL 44 · CPT
*Mon Dec 14*

One therapy teaches you to argue with the lies trauma told you.  
  
It's called Cognitive Processing Therapy. CPT.  
  
Trauma leaves beliefs behind. "It was my fault." "I can't trust anyone."

Those beliefs feel like facts. They're not.  
  
In CPT, you and a therapist size up those thoughts, like you'd size up a scene.  
Is that true? What's the evidence?  
What would you tell a rookie who said that?  
  
It's usually about twelve sessions. There's homework. It's work.  
  
And it's one of the most proven treatments for PTSD there is. The VA uses it every day.  
  
Ask for it by name. CPT.

## CALL 45 · Prolonged Exposure
*Tue Dec 15*

The thing you avoid is the way out.

And the alarm finally turns down.  
  
It's one of the most studied PTSD treatments in the world.  
  
Hard is not the same as harmful.

## CALL 46 · EMDR
*Wed Dec 16*

It sounds weird. It has the research behind it.

A lot of responders and vets like it  
because you don't have to tell the whole story out loud, in detail.  
  
If talking about it feels impossible, ask about EMDR.  
  
Weird is fine. Working is what counts.
