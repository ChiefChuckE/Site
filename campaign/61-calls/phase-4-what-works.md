### CALL 44 · Mon Dec 14 · CPT
**On screen:** One therapy teaches you to argue with the lies trauma told you.

One therapy teaches you to argue with the lies trauma told you.

It's called Cognitive Processing Therapy. CPT.

Trauma leaves beliefs behind. "It was my fault." "I can't trust anyone."
"Nowhere is safe."

Those beliefs feel like facts. They're not.

In CPT, you and a therapist size up those thoughts, like you'd size up a scene.
Is that true? What's the evidence?
What would you tell a rookie who said that?

It's usually about twelve sessions. There's homework. It's work.

And it's one of the most proven treatments for PTSD there is. The VA uses it every day.

Ask for it by name. CPT.

### CALL 45 · Tue Dec 15 · Prolonged Exposure
**On screen:** The thing you avoid is the way out.

The thing you avoid is the way out.

Prolonged Exposure therapy. PE.

Remember avoidance, from Call 7? Every time you dodge the memory,
your brain thinks it's still dangerous.

PE flips that. With a trained therapist, you slowly face the memory.
You tell it. Again and again.
And you face the places you've been avoiding, one step at a time.

It sounds brutal. At first, it's hard.

But your brain learns, "I can remember this, and I'm safe."
And the alarm finally turns down.

It's one of the most studied PTSD treatments in the world.

Hard is not the same as harmful.

### CALL 46 · Wed Dec 16 · EMDR
**On screen:** It sounds weird. The research says it works.

It sounds weird. It has the research behind it.

EMDR. Eye Movement Desensitization and Reprocessing.

You bring up the memory while doing back-and-forth movements.
Following a light or a hand with your eyes. Or taps. Or tones.

Experts still argue about why it works. But study after study says it does.
The VA and the World Health Organization both recommend it.

A lot of responders and vets like it
because you don't have to tell the whole story out loud, in detail.

If talking about it feels impossible, ask about EMDR.

Weird is fine. Working is what counts.

### CALL 47 · Thu Dec 17 · Finding the right one
**On screen:** Your therapist shouldn't flinch at your stories.

Your therapist shouldn't flinch at your stories.

A lot of us try counseling once, get someone who goes pale at the first call we describe,
and never go back.

That's not you failing. That's a bad fit.

Look for someone who knows responders or veterans. Ask them straight.

"Have you worked with responders or vets?"
"Do you do CPT, PE, or EMDR?"
"Can you handle hearing what I've seen?"

Peer support, your union, and the VA can point you to people who get it.

It's okay to try a second one. Or a third.

You wouldn't keep a partner who froze on scene. Don't keep a therapist who does.

### CALL 49 · Sat Dec 19 · Sleep as a weapon
**On screen:** Fix the sleep and you fix half the fight.

Fix the sleep and you fix half the fight.

I told you nightmares are the worst part for me. So let's talk about sleep.

There's a therapy just for nightmares. Imagery Rehearsal Therapy.
While you're awake, you rewrite the ending of the nightmare, and you practice the new one.
For a lot of people, the nightmares ease up.

There's a proven therapy for insomnia too. CBT-I. No pills required.

The basics help. Same wake-up time every day.
No booze to knock you out. Phone out of the bedroom. Dark, cool room.

Sleep isn't lazy. It's where your brain heals.

Protect it like your life depends on it. It might.

### CALL 50 · Sun Dec 20 · Breathe
**On screen:** You already carry the best tool on the rig.

You already carry the best tool on the rig. Your breath.

When the alarm goes off in your body, your breath is the one thing you can grab right now.

Try this. Two breaths in through your nose. One normal, one more on top.
Then one long, slow breath out through your mouth. Three times.

That long breath out tells your body to stand down.

Or box breathing. In for four. Hold for four. Out for four. Hold for four.
Soldiers and SWAT teams use it.

It won't fix PTSD. It'll get you through the next five minutes.

Sometimes the next five minutes is all you need.

Do it with me. Right now.

### CALL 51 · Mon Dec 21 · Move
**On screen:** Your body is built to burn off adrenaline. Let it.

Your body is built to burn off adrenaline. Let it.

Adrenaline is fuel for a fight. When there's no fight, it just sits there.
Making you tight. Wired. Angry.

Exercise burns it off. Research shows regular exercise can ease PTSD symptoms,
depression, and anxiety. And it helps you sleep.

You don't need a fancy gym. Walk the dog. Lift in the garage.
Hit a heavy bag. Swim. Hike.

Twenty or thirty minutes, most days.

With my brain injury, I have to go slower. That's fine. Slow still counts.

And if you can, do it with somebody. Moving next to a buddy is half the therapy.

### CALL 52 · Tue Dec 22 · Know your rights
**On screen:** In some states, PTSD is presumed to come from the job.

In some states, PTSD is presumed to come from the job. Know your law.

It's called a presumption. If you're covered, the law assumes your PTSD is job-related for workers' comp.
California passed one in 2019 for firefighters and peace officers.

Not every state has one. Some cover some jobs and not others.
Dispatchers and EMTs often get left out.

Veterans, PTSD can be service-connected through the VA.
A Veterans Service Officer can help you file. For free.

Don't wait for a crisis to learn this.

Call your union. Call a VSO. Ask, "What am I covered for?"

You were hurt on the job. That's not a favor. That's your right.

### CALL 53 · Wed Dec 23 · Talking to your kids
**On screen:** Your kids already know something's wrong.

Your kids already know something's wrong.

Kids read a room better than any of us. They feel the tension. They see the anger.
They know when you're gone, even sitting on the couch.

And when nobody explains it, kids fill in the blanks.
Usually with, "It's my fault."

You don't need to tell them about the calls. Keep it simple. Fit it to their age.

"My job hurt my brain. It's not your fault. I'm getting help. I love you."

That's it. That's enough.

Then keep showing up. Board games. Rides to practice. Bedtime.

Your kids don't need a perfect parent. They need one who's honest, and coming back.
