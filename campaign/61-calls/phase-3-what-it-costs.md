### CALL 29 · Sun Nov 29 · The number
**On screen:** More of us die by suicide than in the line of duty.

More of us die by suicide than in the line of duty.

Hear that again.

A 2018 study found police and firefighters were more likely to die by suicide than on the job.
And veterans die by suicide at higher rates than people who never served.

We train for the fire. We train for the shooter. We train for the crash.
Almost nobody trains us for this.

I'm not saying this to scare you. I'm saying it because the silence is what kills.

Suicide is preventable. People who were at the edge are alive today
because somebody asked. Somebody stayed. Somebody called.

For the next two weeks, I'm going to show you how to be that somebody.

### CALL 32 · Wed Dec 2 · The joke
**On screen:** Dark humor keeps us sane. Until the joke isn't a joke.

Dark humor keeps us sane. Until the joke isn't a joke.

Every crew has it. The gallows humor no civilian would understand.
It's how we breathe after the worst calls. I'm not against it.

But listen closer.

"If I don't wake up tomorrow, at least I won't have to work this shift."
"You can have my truck when I'm gone."
"Nobody'd notice anyway."

Sometimes that's a joke. Sometimes it's a test,
to see if anyone will hear them.

If a joke about dying gives you a weird feeling in your gut, trust the gut.

Pull them aside. "I heard what you said. Were you serious?"

### CALL 36 · Sun Dec 6 · Ask the question
**On screen:** Ask it out loud: "Are you thinking about killing yourself?"

Ask it out loud. "Are you thinking about killing yourself?"

Not "You're not thinking of doing anything stupid, are you?"
That's asking for a "no."

Ask it straight.

You will not put the idea in their head. Research says asking doesn't raise the risk.
It often brings relief. Somebody finally saw them.

If they say yes, don't panic. Don't lecture. Stay with them.

Help them put time and distance between themselves and anything they could use to get hurt.
Offer to hold their guns for a while.

Then make the call together. 988. Or Safe Call Now.

You don't have to fix it. Ask. Stay. Connect.

### CALL 37 · Mon Dec 7 · What not to say
**On screen:** "You've seen worse." Stop saying that.

"You've seen worse." Stop saying that.

Here's what not to say to a responder or a vet who's struggling.

"You've seen worse."
"Just don't think about it."
"At least you made it home."
"You should be over it by now."

Every one of those says: shut up.

Here's what works.

"That sounds heavy."
"I'm not going anywhere."
"Thanks for telling me."
"What do you need right now?"

You don't need the perfect words.
You need to stay in the room.

### CALL 39 · Wed Dec 9 · Peer support
**On screen:** Sometimes the only person who can reach you has been there too.

Sometimes the only person who can reach you is someone who's been there.

That's peer support. Trained responders and veterans who've walked the same road.
They're not therapists. They're the first door.

They know the calls. They know the culture. You don't have to translate.

Most departments have a team now. If yours doesn't, try your union. Or a neighboring agency.
Veterans can find peers through Vet Centers and veteran groups.

Peer support can't fix everything. But it can walk you to the help that can.

Find out who your peer support person is before you need one.
Put their number in your phone. Today.

### CALL 40 · Thu Dec 10 · Help you already have
**On screen:** There's help you already have and never used.

There's help you already have and probably never used.

Most departments have an Employee Assistance Program. EAP.
It usually gives you a few free, confidential counseling sessions.
Often your family can use it too.

Is it perfect? No. Some EAP counselors don't get responders.
If yours doesn't, ask for one who does.

Veterans, you may qualify for free counseling at a Vet Center.
It's confidential, and many of the counselors are vets themselves.

You earned this help. Using it isn't charity.

Find your EAP number tonight. It's in your HR paperwork or on your department's website.

### CALL 41 · Fri Dec 11 · Will they find out?
**On screen:** "If I get help, will my department find out?"

"If I get help, will my department find out?" Ask the right questions first.

In general, your medical and counseling records are private.
Your employer usually can't see them without your written permission.

But there are exceptions. Fitness-for-duty exams. Certain safety situations.
Rules that differ by state, agency, and contract.

So before your first session, ask the counselor:
"Who can see my records?"
"What would you have to report?"
"Will my employer get anything?"

Ask your union rep the same questions.

Knowing the answers takes away the fear.

This is general information, not legal advice. Get the answers for your own situation.
