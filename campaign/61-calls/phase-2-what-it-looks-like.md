### CALL 16 · Mon Nov 16 · The drink after shift
**On screen:** One beer to come down. Then it's six.

One beer to come down. Then it's six.

After a bad shift, you need to come down. A beer does it. Fast.
Then it takes two. Then six. Then it's every night,
and the morning is worse than the call.

Here's what nobody tells you. Alcohol knocks you out, but it wrecks real sleep.
And it makes the anxiety worse the next day. So you need it more.

That's not a party. That's self-medicating.

We're not drinking for fun. We're drinking to not feel.

If you can't go a week without it, that's your answer.

Talk to someone. Before the bottle talks for you.

### CALL 17 · Tue Nov 17 · Anger first
**On screen:** PTSD doesn't always look like sad. It looks like mad.

For a lot of us, PTSD doesn't look like sad. It looks like mad.

You blow up at the kid for spilling milk.
You want to fight the guy who cut you off.
Your crew walks on eggshells.

Here's why. Your alarm is stuck on. Your body's ready to fight all day.
And in our world, anger is the only feeling that's allowed.
So everything comes out as anger. Grief. Fear. Guilt. All of it.

Anger isn't the problem. It's the smoke.
The fire is underneath.

Next time you blow, ask yourself:
What am I really feeling right now?

### CALL 18 · Wed Nov 18 · Sleep
**On screen:** Shift work breaks your sleep. Trauma finishes the job.

Shift work breaks your sleep. Trauma finishes the job.

Twenty-four on. Tones at two a.m. Watch rotations. Your body never knows what time it is.

Then add PTSD. Your brain won't stand down.
You wake at three with your heart pounding.
You dread sleep because of what's waiting there.

Bad sleep makes everything worse. Anger. Memory. Pain. Drinking. Dark thoughts.

And if you live with a brain injury like I do,
sleep is when your brain does its repair work. No sleep, no repairs.

Sleep isn't a luxury. It's medical.

Tell your doctor you're not sleeping. And tell them why.

**Pinned comment:** How many hours did you actually sleep last night?

### CALL 19 · Thu Nov 19 · Your body is keeping count
**On screen:** Trauma doesn't just live in your head.

Trauma doesn't just live in your head. It lives in your body.

Headaches. A gut that's always in knots.
Back and neck pain that never lets go. Blood pressure through the roof.

When your body stays in fight mode for years, it wears down.
Research links PTSD to a higher risk of heart disease.

We're good at ignoring our bodies. We worked hurt for years.

Stop. Get the physical.
And tell the doctor what you've seen on the job. It's part of your medical history.

If you have chest pain, don't guess. Get it checked today.

Your body has been keeping count. Let somebody read it.

### CALL 21 · Sat Nov 21 · The quiet one
**On screen:** The loudest warning sign is silence.

The loudest warning sign is silence.

He used to give everybody grief at the kitchen table. Now he eats in the truck.
She used to come to every barbecue. Now there's always an excuse.

When someone pulls away, we give them space. We think we're being respectful.

Sometimes space is the worst thing you can give.

Pulling away from people is a sign of PTSD. And depression.
And it can come before a crisis.

So if someone's gone quiet, don't wait for them to come to you. They won't.

Go to them. Sit down. Say,
"You've been quiet. I noticed. What's going on?"

### CALL 23 · Mon Nov 23 · Kid calls
**On screen:** Ask any responder which calls they can't shake. It's the kids.

Ask any responder which calls they can't shake. It's the kids.

### CALL 24 · Tue Nov 24 · Moral injury
**On screen:** Sometimes the wound isn't fear. It's "I couldn't fix it."

Sometimes the wound isn't fear. It's "I couldn't fix it."

You did everything right, and they died anyway.
Or the system failed, and you had to go along with it.
Or you made a call in two seconds that you've questioned for twenty years.

That's called moral injury. Guilt and shame, not just fear.
Veterans and responders carry a lot of it.

It sounds like, "I should have been faster." "It's my fault." "I'm not a good person."

Here's the truth. You made life-and-death decisions with what you had, in seconds.

Hindsight is not a fair judge.

Moral injury needs its own kind of help. Find someone who knows the term.

**Pinned comment:** What does "I should have" sound like in your head?
**Cartoon cut:** a stopwatch at 2 seconds · a man in a courtroom where the judge is labeled "HINDSIGHT" · the gavel set down, the man walking out.

### CALL 25 · Wed Nov 25 · Brain injury or PTSD?
**On screen:** Brain injury and PTSD can look exactly alike. I live with both.

A brain injury and PTSD can look exactly alike. I would know. I live with both.

Trouble remembering. Can't focus. Short fuse. Bad sleep. Headaches.
Noise and bright lights feel like too much.

That list fits a brain injury. It also fits PTSD.

And a lot of us have both. Responders take hits to the head. Veterans take blasts.
Mine came from COVID. My PTSD came from the job.

Here's the danger. A doctor treats one and misses the other.
Or you blame yourself for both.

If you've had a head injury or a serious illness, and you also carry trauma,
ask your doctor to look at both. By name.

That's what this channel is about. Different fire. Same fight.

### CALL 28 · Sat Nov 28 · Five questions
**On screen:** 5 yes-or-no questions. Be honest.

Five yes-or-no questions. Be honest. Think about the past month.

One. Have you had nightmares about it, or thought about it when you didn't want to?

Two. Have you tried hard not to think about it, or gone out of your way to avoid reminders?

Three. Have you been constantly on guard, watchful, or easily startled?

Four. Have you felt numb, or cut off from people and things you used to enjoy?

Five. Have you felt guilty, or unable to stop blaming yourself or others?

Three or more yeses? That's not a diagnosis.
It's a reason to talk to a doctor. This week.

These come from the VA's own screening questions.
